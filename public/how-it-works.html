How it works

A homeowner estimator for solar, a heat pump and a battery, with a CRM lead pipeline behind it. This page shows what to try and what happens behind the scenes.

Built by Garima as part of an application for the GTM Engineer role at Elvy. It's a concept project and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Elvy. Code: github.com/garima0901-source/home-energy-estimator

The demo runs on a built-in CRM simulation, so nobody's real data is touched. It behaves like a real CRM: contacts, deals, notes, tasks and owners. The code also includes a HubSpot connector written against HubSpot's REST API; it hasn't been run against a live portal yet.

What happens when someone submits

  1. The estimate
  2. Validate the inputPostcode, phone number and email are normalised before anything else runs. The CRM only ever sees clean data.
  3. Find the location and price areaPostcode → coordinates (OpenStreetMap) → price area SE1–SE4.
  4. Fetch live dataThe last 7 days of spot prices for that area (elprisetjustnu.se, Nord Pool data), and solar yield for that exact location (EU JRC PVGIS).
  5. Run the savings and CO₂ modelHeat demand from size and build year, today's heating and electricity cost, then the same household with a heat pump, solar and a battery. Every assumption is in one file.
  6. Score the leadRule-based, with the reasons written on the contact: heating type, savings, timeline, price area, phone number left.
  7. Write the words with AIClaude writes the homeowner's summary in Swedish or English and a pre-call brief for the salesperson. It explains the numbers; it doesn't invent them.
  8. The CRM
  9. Look up the contact by emailEmail is the identity. A returning homeowner updates their record.
  10. Assign an ownerRouted by region. A contact who already has an owner keeps them.
  11. Create or update the contact19 typed custom properties. Lifecycle stage is only set on new contacts, because HubSpot can't move it backwards.
  12. Create or update one dealHot and warm leads get a deal in the "Homeowner estimates" pipeline. An existing open deal is updated, not duplicated. Nurture leads and renters don't get one.
  13. Attach the sales briefAs a note on the contact and deal: why they might buy, likely objections, an opening line, things to check on the call.
  14. Create a follow-up taskWith an SLA that matches the lead (table below).
  15. Notify the teamHot leads also post to a Slack-compatible webhook.

Why it's built this way

  • The numbers are deterministic; AI only writes the words. A salesperson can explain every number to a homeowner, and an analyst can change any assumption in one file.
  • Honest when it doesn't pay off. Telling a district-heating household the truth builds more trust than a lead they'd cancel later.
  • The pipeline only holds real opportunities. Curious visitors are contacts with a follow-up, not deals that distort the forecast.
  • Scoring is rules, not a model. Sales can see why a lead is hot and argue with the weights.
  • The CRM serves the salesperson. The brief and the task are waiting where they already work, before the first call.
  • Nothing breaks when a dependency is down. Each external call has a timeout, a cache and a fallback. With no AI key the text comes from a template; with no HubSpot token the in-memory CRM behaves the same way.
  • GDPR from the start. Explicit consent, stored with a timestamp on the contact, and no personal data in URLs.

Data and assumptions

  • Spot prices: elprisetjustnu.se API (Nord Pool day-ahead, 15-minute resolution).
  • Solar yield: EU JRC PVGIS, kWh per installed kWp per year at the address.
  • Location: OpenStreetMap Nominatim.
  • The model uses reference annual spot prices per area for the yearly cost, and shows this week's live price alongside. Heat demand, heating costs and efficiencies are standard Swedish values, all listed in src/config.ts.
  • The subscription comparison uses the publicly reported price of SEK 2,500 per month. It's an indicative estimate, not a quote.
